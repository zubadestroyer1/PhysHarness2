# Exponential-decay pilot — results

**Status, 24 September 2026:** Three single-agent calibrations and all four final matched two-agent trials completed with independently kernel-checked proofs. One earlier calibration failed before candidate submission and was retired with its cost retained. A historical two-agent no-sharing comparison completed with verified proofs; a historical ideas-sharing comparison produced two verified root proofs but failed during workspace export. Both historical comparisons are excluded from the final matched comparison. The completed pilot does **not** establish a sharing-policy benefit.

The target is a known damped-oscillator energy-decay bound for arbitrary positive mass, stiffness, and damping. This is a private development exercise, not an open-result claim. An assistant reviewed the problem's mathematical faithfulness; that review is not human-expert or publication approval. The withheld reference and adversarial controls passed pinned Comparator and independent-kernel checks. An accepted worker receipt gives a kernel result for the bound target under the frozen challenge and environment; it does not by itself validate physical modelling assumptions or publishability.

All three completed calibrations have one accepted proof receipt each, no direct messages, no recorded context compaction, zero outstanding reservations, and zero active workers at terminal audit. Their proofs arrived in about 4.4–4.6 minutes, below the intended 10–30-minute difficulty range; these runs therefore do not exercise long-horizon search or context compaction. The original third calibration hit a command-validation fault before dispatch and submitted no candidate. The operator archived its evidence, retired the workspace through the audited recovery path, and kept **$0.860240** of settled cost in every budget audit. Historical comparison 4 had two accepted root proofs and a completed detached helper task. Historical comparison 5 also produced two independently kernel-checked root proofs. This was an operational failure, not a proof rejection or OOM: the container was observed running with `OOMKilled=false` and zero memory-event counters. The detached helper never obtained a model session. After preserving the workspace archive, the operator recovered the workspace, settled all 59 reservations, and cancelled the experiment. Its **$5.131777** ledger spend remains counted; no completed whole-attempt time is reported. The global-sort repair and a focused regression test address the observed cause. See `comparison-5-incident.md` for the content-free evidence record.

The no-sharing replacement used 70 settled native responses, 1,729,667 tokens, and had no direct messages or recorded compaction. Its two root proofs were independently verified, while its detached helper completed after both proofs; sharing-policy rejections did not become useful root-to-root communication. The ideas-sharing replacement used 61 settled responses, 1,775,311 tokens, and had no recorded compaction. It had two actual parent/helper messages, both acknowledged, plus one resolved parent wait-for-helper handoff to a successor session; there was no root-to-root message, and the reported feedback arrived after both root proofs. These observations distinguish the policy setting from communication that could have affected first-proof time. They do not establish a causal advantage from two trials.

After comparison 5 completed, an operator-only handoff validator was amended to recognize a hash-checked consumed handoff followed by a settled terminal successor. The source-freeze amendment records no change to model-facing research runtime, challenge, or worker tools. It covers zero or one handoff per task; repeated handoffs on the same task still require manual lineage review. The amendment passed 35 focused tests, and comparison 5's 584 exported artifacts were checked. This wrapper version change is disclosed when comparing the final trials, even though the scientific runtime remained unchanged.

Trial 6 used 79 settled native responses and 2,739,716 tokens. Its two root proofs were independently verified, with no recorded compaction or handoff. Six direct messages were created and all six message items were acknowledged through four deliveries; there were no discussion posts. Trial 7 used 84 settled responses and 2,804,141 tokens. Its two root proofs were independently verified, with no direct messages or recorded compaction. The no-sharing policy rejected two attempted messages and a detached helper return outside its allowed scope; one discussion post had no acknowledged delivery.

Across the final matched trials, the two ideas-sharing first-proof times averaged 281.46 seconds and the two no-sharing times averaged 298.62 seconds. Whole-attempt times averaged 375.27 versus 428.29 seconds, and settled ceiling ledgers averaged $5.935993 versus $5.964485, respectively. These are **descriptive observations from two trials per setting**, not a causal or general performance estimate. The communication timeline found no root-to-root exchange, and the communicating helper feedback arrived after proof submissions. Thus the observed time differences cannot be attributed to timely sharing in this pilot. None of the four trials recorded a native context compaction.

The final ledger audit finds **10 attempts drained**, including the two historical comparison attempts and failed calibration, and **15 verified receipts** including historical ones. New conservative settled spend is **$40.104875**; adding **$3.658018** of prior spend gives **$43.762893 aggregate**, below the $92 new and $100 aggregate caps. The audit retains both failures; retirement or replacement did not reset their cost. All reservations and active worker slots are drained. Price-based bill estimates in the tables are lower than the ceiling ledger and are not vendor invoices.

The configured model was GPT-6 Sol at high reasoning effort, with a 256,000-token active context limit and a 64,000-token per-response output limit. There was **no lifetime token limit**. Each attempt had a 45-minute research execution limit; already queued independent verification could add supervisor drain time. The known target was solved quickly, so this pilot did not test sustained reasoning, repeated context compaction and resume, or hard-search collaboration. It used a local VM setup, not cloud deployment or larger fleets. Operator validation covers at most one helper handoff per task; repeated same-task handoffs require manual review.

The next useful experiment needs a harder independently qualified target: the proposed driven-oscillator fallback remains unqualified. It should provide timely helper capacity and a lifecycle that can return useful feedback before proof submission, with clearer tool guidance. Repeated compaction and resume should be tested before expanding to larger fleets. These are design priorities inferred from this pilot, not demonstrated improvements.

The final host suite passed **1,143 tests, with 13 skipped and 21 warnings**. The earlier pinned-VM library checks passed 13/13. Verifier qualification attempt 04 passed all 10 mechanical checks; a separate real-VM archive round-trip regression also passed, and the 147-file source freeze still matched. After auditing, the dedicated VM had zero containers and was confirmed stopped at 10:53:24 UTC; the pilot heartbeat was paused. These checks support the pilot machinery, not a claim that the result generalizes to all proof systems or long-horizon research.
