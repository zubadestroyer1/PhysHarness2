# Final design audit — exponential pilot

Read-only audit of the 24 September 2026 manifest and registry assembled in `.state/exponential-pilot-2026-09-24`. This checks experiment identity, accounting and interpretation; it does not inspect credentials or withheld proof contents, and it does not qualify the planned runs.

## Setup checked

- The manifest has seven active slots: three single-root calibrations at $12 each, followed by four two-root comparison slots at $14 each in `none / ideas / ideas / none` order. Active slots use distinct project IDs, experiment IDs, databases, artifact stores and private directories. Three failed or superseded paid attempts remain separately listed as retired. Registry v3 has seven routes, each naming the corresponding active problem revision, pinned verifier image and an attempt-private bundle. Each database problem matches its attempt's target digest and the common challenge hash. The bundles inspected by path contain no named solution or prior-run transcript. The worker image is a pinned derivative of the qualified base image; its Dockerfile does not copy host `.state` or `work` content. These observations support the intended isolation, but cannot rule out model prior knowledge, all hidden leakage channels, or an operator accidentally reusing prior text in a prompt.
- Each active experiment's prepared contract fixes GPT-6 Sol at high reasoning effort, concurrency one for calibration and two for comparison, 2,700-second research runtime, $12/$14 cost envelope, 256,000-token active context, 64,000-token per-response output, no lifetime token limit, and 1,000 maximum turns. The research compaction rule yields a threshold of 183,808 tokens (`min(3×256,000/4, 256,000−64,000−8,192)`). This does **not** guarantee that any run will compact or hand off. The supervisor may then drain already queued verification for up to 600 more seconds; report that actual wall time separately from model/task time and first-proof time.
- The current machine calibration decision exactly matches the runner's three-key `continue` gate; its rich rationale is stored separately. Registry v3 exists with seven scoped entries, and the deployment decision binds its current SHA-256. The regenerated source freeze matched all 145 collected files in a read-only `launch_frozen.py check` against the current manifest and qualification packet. At the time of this note, the next comparison slots remain unstarted.

## Accounting and protocol deviations

The fixed planned-slot sum is $36 + $56 = $92, within the original $100 aggregate ceiling after $3.658018 prior spending. The runner's prelaunch audit reopens **both active and retired ledgers**, rejects uncertain costs or nonterminal retired work, and tests actual settled plus held cost plus the next full attempt ceiling against both the $92 new-spend and $100 aggregate caps. At this audit snapshot, active plus retired pilot ledgers contain **$16.303920 settled** and zero held cost: three accepted calibrations ($5.031980), failed original calibration 3 ($0.860240), and retired original comparisons 4 and 5 ($10.411700). With the next $14 reservation, projected new conservative accounting is $30.303920, and prior plus new is $33.961938. Failure and retirement did not refund model spend. Dollar values here are conservative ceiling-ledger charges, not provider invoices; local VM/development time is outside them.

The original comparison 4 (`none`) and comparison 5 (`ideas`) each produced an independently accepted first proof before being retired for operational reasons. Their first-proof times were 261.08s and 275.71s; their final conservative ledgers were $5.279923 and $5.131777. The fresh replacements preserve project and filesystem isolation, but they do not erase these observed attempts. Report the original outcomes, exclusion reasons and costs alongside the four planned clean slots. A per-protocol two-per-policy comparison may be useful descriptively; an all-paid-attempt sensitivity view is necessary to show how retries affect the result. All three completed calibrations found accepted proofs in less than five minutes (275.23s, 273.92s and 264.59s), with a fourth paid calibration attempt lost to a harness fault. The predeclared median first-proof time above ten minutes was **not met**. The retained target is suitable for an operational comparison of this workflow, not a long-horizon difficulty claim. It is a known result chosen and repaired after development feedback, not an independent holdout or a novel/open theorem.

## Interpretation limits

Two repetitions per sharing policy cannot estimate a reliable collaboration effect or rare-failure rate. Compare first accepted proof time and *settled cost at that receipt* as primary endpoints; report whole-attempt time/cost, actual delivered discussion, direct messages, delegation, source retrieval, compaction and handoffs separately. An `ideas` setting without timely delivered help is a configuration comparison, not demonstrated collaborative assistance. Earlier accepted proofs are blocked from canonical retrieval by separate projects, but arm order, repeated target exposure, runtime changes after faults and operator learning remain possible confounders.

The two-root comparison fills the `max_concurrency=2` workspace/worker slots. An optional helper therefore waits for a root to exit; in the original comparison 4 it ran only after the proofs. This design cannot assess whether a timely extra agent would help while both roots are active. A subsequent study should first select and independently qualify a target whose single-agent calibration takes roughly 10–30 minutes, then compare an equal-budget flat independent baseline against a condition with three or four slots, or a bounded parent-yield policy that frees a slot for a helper. That is a future design recommendation, not a change to this frozen pilot.

## Tool-awareness follow-up

The completed comparison 4 retry again exposed two expected permission outcomes: a detached helper attempted `return_result` and received `RETURN_RESULT_SCOPE`; cross-branch `send_message` under `sharing=none` received `SHARING_POLICY`. The guards are correct. `return_result` is only for a joined child with a `reply_to_parent_task_id`; detached work has no parent return channel. Cross-branch free-text messages require `ideas` sharing. These errors therefore do not indicate a privacy or attribution bypass.

The worker-facing guidance is less clear than the guards. The `return_result` tool says “joined parent,” but it is offered to every task, including detached helpers. The helper's prompt carries its objective and a branch-local `research_brief`; the brief's open-task entries include objective and dependencies but omit `detached` and `reply_to_parent_task_id`. The detached helper's objective in the retry did not itself mention detached status or the return contract. The `send_message` description says only that it sends an attributed idea; generic research instructions encourage peer messages. The brief carries the raw `sharing=none` value, but there is no direct warning in the tool description that cross-branch sends will be refused. These are avoidable affordance/prompt gaps, even though an agent could infer parts of the policy and the service rejects the calls safely.

Roots receive `research_capacity` in their initial prompt and can call the tool again. It reports queued/running task counts plus `active_workers` and `max_concurrency`; `child_task_status` can also show a known delegated helper's queued/running/terminal status. Neither reports a queue position or expected start time. `delegate_detached` says its work will not delay the parent's final response, but does not warn that, with two active roots and two slots, the helper will queue until a slot opens and may continue consuming the shared budget after a proof. Thus the root has capacity and status data to reason from, but no explicit scheduling consequence at delegation time. For a future non-frozen run, expose the current task's joined/detached return contract and sharing policy in the prompt; restrict or annotate `return_result` and `send_message` according to that contract; and make delegation feedback state whether the new task is queued behind full capacity. Treat the observed rejections as tool-awareness evidence, not as a collaboration success or failure measure.

## Comparison 5 retry: joined wait and successor

Canonical events in the completed `ideas` retry show one **joined** helper, not a detached helper. It was queued at 10:20:27 UTC. Both roots occupied the two available slots, so it was leased at 10:24:38, 69 milliseconds after the *other* root completed and freed a slot. The two root proof submissions were already queued for independent verification at 10:24:02 and 10:24:23. The first receipt was verified at 10:24:32 and the parent's own receipt at 10:25:01. The helper therefore began after both submitted candidates and could not have contributed to either proof submission; its recorded result came after both acceptances.

The parent sent one attributed message to the helper at 10:25:07, then requested `wait_for_tasks` handoff at 10:25:14. The helper recorded a typed `return_result` at 10:25:15 with `evidence_status=unverified` and one artifact, completed at 10:25:18, and the service created the attributed child-to-parent return message. These are the run's **two** direct messages, one in each parent/helper direction; there is no root-to-root message. Both message deliveries were acknowledged. The parent had a `handed_off` source session, was re-leased under a new fence at 10:25:18, restored a successor workspace at 10:25:20, and completed at 10:25:40. Its canonical continuation names `wait_for_tasks`, the exact child ID and source checkpoint digest, and `continuation_mode=portable`; the successor has a distinct native session ID. The evaluator reports zero active-context or provider compactions.

This demonstrates a functioning joined-child return, explicit wait boundary, capacity release, and checkpoint-bound successor completion. It does **not** demonstrate that the parent's yield made capacity for the helper—the other root's completion did that before the yield—or that helper feedback improved either accepted proof. A portable handoff into another native session is a different mechanism from provider context compaction and does not by itself establish long-horizon performance.

## Comparison 6: detached-helper timing

The final `none` comparison completed both independent root receipts and all three tasks. The detached helper was queued by one root soon after start but could not occupy a third slot while both roots ran. Root B submitted at 10:38:41 UTC, was verified at 10:39:11, and completed at 10:39:17; only then, 68 milliseconds later, was the helper leased. Root A had sent two messages to the queued helper at 10:35:43 and 10:38:01, and their delivery was acknowledged when the helper started at 10:39:17. The helper sent its first reply at 10:40:10.534, 258 milliseconds before Root A's proof submission at 10:40:10.792, but that reply's **delivery was acknowledged at 10:40:11.000, after submission**. The helper's two further replies were created at 10:40:24 and 10:40:37, also after submission; Root A's proof was verified at 10:40:40. Root A sent one final message to the helper at 10:40:46, and the helper completed at 10:41:11.

Thus the canonical record supports an actual six-message, acknowledged parent/helper exchange, with no root-to-root message and no typed `return_result` (the helper was detached). It does not support helper feedback affecting either submitted proof: Root B had already been accepted before the helper started, and Root A's first helper reply was delivered only after its own submission. The near-simultaneous first reply and Root A submission should not be counted as pre-proof collaboration. The two-slot capacity ceiling again made the helper late rather than a concurrent source of ideas for either proof.
