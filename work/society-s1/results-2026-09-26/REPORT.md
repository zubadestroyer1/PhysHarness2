# S1 live comparison: results (2026-09-26)

## Summary

- **All 13 completed arms proved their target.**
  - Each first proof submission was accepted by the qualified verifier pipeline (Comparator, Lean and nanoda) against the exact frozen statement.
  - No arm fetched literature, and no contamination flag was raised.
- **Both candidate targets were too easy to separate the arms.** One agent proved the Doeblin target in 6.3 minutes. On the harder aperiodic target, every independent run finished in 12–17 minutes. The comparison therefore measures time and cost to a verified proof, not whether a proof is found.
- **On the aperiodic target, the society was slower and more expensive than independent agents.**
  - Society (6 roots, 8 workers): 29.7 minutes and $155.41.
  - The 8 independent one-root runs: median 14.0 minutes and $36.02.
  - The single-agent arm: 13.9 minutes and $31.97.
  - The society was slower than every independent run, and cost 4.3 times the independent median.
- **The society's overhead is visible:**
  - referee reviews took 34 of its 40 task slots;
  - 55% of its tool calls went to the shared commons;
  - its 8 workers ran against the provider's 2M tokens-per-minute organization limit.

  This is one society run on one easy target, so it is not a general research-performance claim.
- **Stability on the Mac:** the host memory level never fell below 73 (100 is fully free; the watchdog stops the VM below 20 and warns below 35). There were no out-of-memory events and no workbench crashes. Total spend was $568.29, against a plan of $2,400.

### How the society behaved (S-r2)

- **Structure:** 6 agents in 6 labs, plus 34 referee tasks.
  - Reviews: 23 faithful, 10 sound, 1 unfaithful. No referee verdict was negative.
  - 154 citations, 29 of them across branches, and 1 cross-branch dependency.
  - 22 claims were formally stated and 5 were abandoned.
- **Tool calls:** 55% went to the commons (claims, posts, reads and nodes), against about 33% in the one-root runs. The Lean formalization share of mathematical work was 0.70, against a median of 0.39 for I. It ran 169 Lean checks, against a median of 24 for I.
- **Throttling:** 8 workers need about 2.5M tokens per minute, above the 2M organization limit. After the fix, refusals wait and resend rather than failing. Part of S-r2's time is therefore throughput throttling that the arms at concurrency 2 did not hit.

### Interpretation

On a target that one agent proves in about 14 minutes, the society's coordination costs dominate: referee review, commons traffic and a shared rate limit. Those costs buy nothing a lone agent needs.

This result does not show whether a society helps on a problem that single agents fail. S1 could not test that, because neither candidate target was hard enough. The next comparison needs a target that independent single agents fail within their budget.

## Stability and operations

- **Memory:** a host watchdog sampled `kern.memorystatus_level` every 20 seconds throughout.
  - Minimum 73, including the 8-worker society arm (89 or more during S-r2).
  - No warnings (below 35), no stop (below 20), no OOM kills.
  - Each arm pre-warmed the olean page cache. The dedicated VM was stopped at the end.
- **Accounting:** every completed arm drained cleanly: 0 reserved, 0 active workers and 0 unsettled reservations.
  - The two replaced attempts keep zero-cost `uncertain` reservations as an audit record: 4 in the calibration attempt, and 13 (with $16.64 reserved) in S attempt 1.
  - Their local-workspace retirement (`docs/LOCAL_WORKSPACE_RECOVERY.md`) is still pending.
- **Budget amendment:**
  - The user authorized raising calibration-aperiodic's ceiling from $80 to $200 during the run.
  - It was applied through the audited `amend_live_budget` helper from #28: revision-fenced, idempotent, with the authorization recorded in event `experiment.budget.amended`. The helper's $100 cap was set to exactly the authorized amount for that one call.
  - The run finished at $34.61, so the extra headroom went unused.
- **Exports:** every arm was exported and hash-validated (`artifact_integrity_checked`), up to 47,523 artifacts per arm. The exports and databases are kept privately.

## Defects found and fixes

1. **Fixed here: a provider rate-limit refusal failed the task.**
   - **Cause:** `ResponsesRuntime` builds its client with `max_retries=0`. An HTTP 429 `rate_limit_exceeded` raised out of the turn, which left the session `uncertain` and blocked the task.
   - **Fix:** the runtime now resends only that refusal, within the same operation and reservation. The provider refused the request before doing any work, so nothing was generated or charged.
   - **Wait:** the provider's `retry-after-ms` / `retry-after` hint, or a doubling fallback from 1 second. Each wait is capped at 30 seconds and never runs past the turn deadline.
   - **Unchanged:** `insufficient_quota` and every other error keep their existing path.
   - **Tests:** three regressions in `tests/test_execution_responses.py`. The resend test fails without the fix.
   - **Scope:** this file is not among the 61 inputs the verifier qualification freezes, so no requalification is needed.
2. **Follow-up: `phys export` times out on large arms.** `HarnessClient` has a 30-second timeout, and the export manifest of an arm with about 10,000 artifacts takes longer, so the export fails with `TRANSPORT_UNCERTAIN`. The run used a local-service export with the same manifest, hash checks and `validate_export`. The CLI needs a timeout option or a streamed export.
3. **Follow-up: a mid-run pause cannot be resumed on the same database.** Cancelled tasks leave workspaces `reconciliation_required`, sessions `uncertain` and worker slots counted as active. At concurrency 2, no worker can then be admitted. The runbook's answer is a replacement attempt; a supported reconcile-and-resume path would avoid that.
4. **Follow-up: the launcher.** `run-team` cannot pass the RUN_PLAN workforce caps, because `configure_workforce` needs an already-started experiment. It also defaults `max_verifications` to 8. The runs used a launcher that follows `run-team` step for step with both settings.
5. **Follow-up: the budget amendment helper.** Its ceiling cap is a module constant ($100). It should be an explicit, audited parameter.
6. **Observed: tool rejections.** Agents were rejected for `command_rejected`, `CONTEXT_EVIDENCE_KIND`, `CROSS_LAB_MESSAGE`, `NOT_FOUND`, `TASK_TOTAL_CAP` and `REVIEW_PRECONDITION`. Each was recoverable, and there were no stagnation warnings. These are candidates for clearer tool guidance.

## Limits

- **Sample size:** S is a single run. I gives 8 samples of one-root behaviour; single is one more sample of the same configuration with a larger budget.
- **Target difficulty:** both targets are within one agent's reach, so the arms cannot differ in whether they solve it.
- **Throttling:** the provider rate limit slowed S, and only S.
- **Coverage:** one model family was used, and the verifier and workbench qualifications are engineering evidence only.
- **No other claims:** no research-novelty, publication or production claim is made.
